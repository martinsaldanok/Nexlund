Who we are

Closing the distance between technology and the business.

We're living through a moment when AI and other easily available tools let you run a business far faster, more automated and more scalable. Everyone can see that. The real challenge for an owner is a different one: how to bring that technology down into daily operations — especially when it means stepping into a world you don't know, and when the day-to-day leaves no time to work out which technologies deliver and which don't.

Nexlund isn't about implementing one particular technology. It's about accompanying and supporting the process of digitalisation and automation — closing the distance between technology and the business.

What I want is for artificial intelligence to stop being a challenge and become a responsible amplifier for the people using it. Responsible means two things: that you understand what the tool does and why, and that human judgement stays in the decisions that matter. It isn't about handing your business over to a machine; it's about getting the repetitive work off your plate so you have time for what actually needs thought.

What drives us

Three convictions that shape every project.

Owners, not IT departments

The people I talk to are owners, managers and founders. If something can't be explained in a thirty-minute conversation, it's been explained badly.

Small and done right

I'd rather have a simple tool the team uses every day than an ambitious system abandoned after three months.

AI as an amplifier, not a hurdle

The tool should amplify the person — not replace them, and not push them out of their own decisions.

How we work

The work, concretely.

What I enjoy most is the moment someone sees their own business running better. A pilates studio that stops writing bookings in a notebook. A family business that wins back ten hours of admin a week. A founder who finally knows, on the 5th of the month, what they billed the month before. These aren't spectacular transformations — they're small, durable changes that show up in the daily life of the people running the business.

I don't sell software and I don't represent any vendor. When a project grows beyond what I can solve myself, I bring in vetted specialists in AI, automation or e-commerce and stay on the client's side, translating. That independence is deliberate: it's what lets me recommend what actually fits.

I work out of Berlin, but digitally. That lets me support clients who are far away — in other countries too — without distance changing the way we work.
